## BRIEFLY

**What happened.** The European Union's official ETIAS portal says the travel-authorisation system is not operating and is not collecting applications. Reports in July said its planned late-2026 launch was slipping into 2027, but the EU has not announced a new start date.

**What it means.** Visa-exempt visitors do not need to buy ETIAS today. A website offering to sell an active authorisation is not the official application channel, however polished it looks.

**Risks and impact.** Paying early can expose passport details, contact information and card data as well as money. Waiting until the airport after a real launch could create a different problem: carriers will check authorisation before boarding.

**What can be done.** Bookmark the EU portal, check the rule for the passport you will actually use, and ignore countdowns that are not tied to an official start notice.

**What to watch.** The EU says it will announce the specific date several months in advance. When ETIAS begins, it will cost EUR 20 for most applicants, attach electronically to one travel document and remain separate from the border officer's decision.

## FACTS

ETIAS stands for the European Travel Information and Authorisation System. It is designed for people from visa-exempt countries making short visits to 30 European countries. It is not a visa and it is not an application that travellers can submit now.

That last point is the one that matters on 3 October 2026. The official EU page states that ETIAS is currently not in operation and that no applications are being collected. It also says the EU will announce the specific launch date several months before operations begin.

When the system does open, the application will be available through the official ETIAS website or official mobile application. The authorisation will be linked to the passport or other travel document used in the application. The general validity period is three years or until that document expires, whichever comes first.

The fee is no longer the early figure of EUR 7 that still survives in old explainers. Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1411 replaced it with EUR 20 per application. Some applicants, including people under 18 or over 70, are exempt from the fee, but they will still need an authorisation if the requirement otherwise applies to them.

## EVIDENCE

The timetable has changed repeatedly, which is why a remembered date is not enough. On 7 July 2026, the Financial Times reported that the EU was set to move ETIAS into 2027 after problems with the separate Entry/Exit System. Euronews reported the same development the next day, while noting that the delay had not yet been formally posted as a new official date.

The public-facing signal is now unambiguous even without a replacement date: the EU's own portal says the service is not operational. That is stronger evidence for a traveller's immediate decision than an airline blog, search result or commercial application service promising to be ready.

The legal fee is real. The regulation was adopted on 16 July 2025, published in the Official Journal on 29 October and is in force. But a valid price does not make a payment page valid before the system opens.

The future process is also a screening layer, not a border pass. EU guidance says a successful authorisation does not guarantee admission. Border authorities may still verify the passport, purpose and conditions of the stay, and the familiar Schengen rule that a travel document normally remain valid for at least three months after the intended departure and have been issued within the previous ten years.

## PERSPECTIVES

For a traveller, the confusion is understandable. The name resembles the US ESTA, the fee has been enacted, and official pages already explain the application. Those pieces make ETIAS feel live even when the transaction itself does not exist. A trip months away also creates pressure to settle every document early.

For a fraudulent or opportunistic intermediary, that uncertainty is valuable. A convincing page can copy official language, calculate a future fee, add a service charge and collect identity data. It need not issue a real authorisation to cause harm. Passport number, date of birth, address, email and payment details make a richer target than the price alone suggests.

For airlines and other carriers, ETIAS will eventually become a pre-boarding check. Their operational incentive is to prevent travellers without the required status from reaching the border. But carriers cannot enforce a system before it starts, and an airline reminder is not a substitute for the official eligibility rules.

For border authorities, authorisation answers only a preliminary question: did the traveller clear automated and, where necessary, manual pre-travel screening? Admission remains a separate decision made under entry law. That distinction is why a traveller can hold an authorisation and still be refused, or be asked to show accommodation, onward plans or sufficient means.

## CONTEXT

International travel increasingly separates permission into layers. A passport identifies the traveller. A visa or visa exemption determines the broad route. An electronic authorisation allows pre-travel screening. A carrier checks whether it may transport the passenger. A border officer decides whether the entry conditions are met on arrival.

The layers are related, but none collapses all the others. ETIAS will be linked to the exact travel document entered in the application. Renewing a passport can therefore end the usefulness of an otherwise unexpired authorisation. A spelling mismatch or a second nationality can matter because the carrier is checking the document presented for that journey, not the traveller in the abstract.

ETIAS is also distinct from the Entry/Exit System, or EES. EES records crossings and biometrics for many non-EU short-stay travellers; ETIAS is the prior authorisation for eligible visa-exempt travellers. Reporting on the delay matters because ETIAS depends on the wider border technology functioning, but a slow EES queue is not proof that an individual needs to buy ETIAS today.

The safest mental model is a sequence of gates. The first gate is whether the requirement has begun. The second is whether it applies to your nationality, status, destination and purpose. The third is whether the application is genuine and linked to the correct passport. The last gate is the border. Paying attention to the order prevents both premature payments and false confidence.

## PEOPLE

The travellers most exposed to bad information are often those trying hardest to comply: families arranging several passports, older relatives relying on forwarded messages, and people planning an expensive trip far ahead. Urgency turns caution into a cost.

One person may see an advertisement, another may complete the form, and a third may book the flights. Unless they compare the passport number and official rule together, a small mismatch can survive until check-in. Children and older adults may be fee-exempt later, but “fee-exempt” will not mean “application-exempt.”

Travel agents can help organise documents, but responsibility is still document-specific. A useful agent should be able to name the government source, explain whether the system is live and separate an official charge from its own service fee. If the answer begins with artificial scarcity—apply today or lose your place—that is a reason to stop.

## DEEPER

The practical ETIAS audit takes five minutes. Start at the EU's `europa.eu` travel portal rather than a sponsored search result. Confirm that applications are open; on 3 October 2026, they are not. Check whether the passport holder is in scope, because residence rights, visas and special travel documents can change the answer. Note the passport expiry date and use the same document for the future application and journey.

When the launch is officially announced, do not treat “usually processed quickly” as “always instant.” Apply with enough time to correct an error or respond to a request for more information. Save the confirmation, but do not assume a screenshot replaces the electronic link to the passport.

Then perform the separate border check. Confirm the destination's passport-validity rule, the permitted length and purpose of stay, and any evidence you may be asked to show. If the trip involves Ireland, residence in an EU country, a non-standard passport or family rights under EU law, read the specific exception rather than extrapolating from a general list.

The larger lesson is useful beyond Europe: a real future rule can support a fake present transaction. Verify three things in order—Is it live? Does it apply to me? Is this the official channel? That sequence is cheaper than recovering money, replacing a compromised passport or arguing at check-in.
