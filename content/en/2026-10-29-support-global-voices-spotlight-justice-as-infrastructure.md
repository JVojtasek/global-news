Often portrayed as abstract, large, formal, distant, and quite technical, justice is a moral and ethical area, tied to institutions and legal statutes, and can seem a long way from mundane daily experience. But it is also a deeply practical underpinning for how most societies function. Without justice systems that work and are understood at the most fundamental level, economic mobility is threatened, socially marginalized groups have no recourse, and lack of trust hampers institutions and interpersonal interactions alike.

Justice is the grease in the motor of social functioning. Nobody will contest the importance of justice to holding people to a collective moral code of conduct, but the often overlooked next-level function justice plays is that, when justice is fair, fast, and reliable, it makes everything in a society run better. It allows for a society to fulfill its economic, social, and creative potential. Copyright law protects ideas and innovation; land law protects resources and livelihoods; labor law supports humane and effective use of our work — as long as those laws are enforced. When you know that it will be straightforward and predictable to get all the permits, you are more likely to start a business. When you know that fraud will be punished, you are more likely to invest your capital. When you know that you will be caught, you’re less likely to evade taxes. When you know that there will be fair and fast prosecution, you are more likely to report injustice and less likely to perpetrate it.

In this spotlight, Global Voices is partnering with The Hague Institute for Innovation of Law (HiiL) to make visible the ways in which justice functions as critical infrastructure for any society, and how countries with a justice deficit can work towards stronger protections that will enable economic and social benefits.
