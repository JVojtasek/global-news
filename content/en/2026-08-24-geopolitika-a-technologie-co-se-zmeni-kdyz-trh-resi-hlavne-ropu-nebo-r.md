With geopolitical news like this, watch whether the market is mainly focused on oil and yields, or on a pure flight from risk; the impact on tech is different in each scenario.

Tech is not stumbling today because of a faulty app, but because of geopolitics dressed up as economic policy. Investors are waiting for details of a pressure campaign against Iran, which the excerpt links to Scott Bessent; for now they do not know the exact mechanism, only the theme: sanctions, financing, oil, shipping and nerves.

The most interesting thing about this story is that tech stocks can, in the short term, become a trade on the oil price, even though most of them do not own a single tanker and have never seen a barrel, not even at a team-building event.

The mechanism has two different branches, and it is important not to mix them. The first is inflation and rates: harder pressure on Iran may raise the geopolitical premium in oil. More expensive energy can then worsen the inflation outlook, which keeps bond yields higher. And higher yields hurt growth tech above all, because a large part of its value lies in profits expected far in the future. It is like buying a flat that will only start earning properly in ten years: when the mortgage gets more expensive, you are suddenly less willing to overpay for the lovely view from the balcony.

The second branch is pure risk aversion: if the market starts to worry about escalation, it may sell highly valued stocks simply because they are liquid and sensitive to crowd mood. In that case yields do not have to rise; investors are just cutting exposure to the most expensive parts of the market. Same headline, different engine. And for the QMA framework, this is exactly the difference between the macro rates pillar and the crowd behaviour pillar.

### Who it helps and who it hurts

It may hurt mainly long-duration growth segments: chipmakers such as NVIDIA (NVDA), AMD (AMD) and Broadcom (AVGO), and cloud and software such as Microsoft (MSFT), Amazon (AMZN) or Alphabet (GOOGL). Not because Iran decides demand for AI servers, but because higher yields and nerves shorten the market's patience.

Cybersecurity and networking companies face a mixed impact. Cloudflare (NET), Palo Alto Networks (PANW) or CrowdStrike (CRWD) may benefit from greater attention to digital risks, but they are also among the stocks sensitive to valuations. In other words: a good story for revenue does not automatically protect the valuation multiple.

Energy may benefit if a geopolitical premium appears in oil: producers such as Exxon Mobil (XOM) and Chevron (CVX), or service companies such as SLB (SLB). Airlines such as Delta Air Lines (DAL) and United Airlines (UAL), on the other hand, may suffer, because more expensive fuel eats into their margins.

A geopolitical premium in oil is like a taxi raising its fare in the rain even before the storm actually starts. People pay extra for the possibility that there will be a problem. For the market, this means: more expensive oil can raise costs, complicate the fight against inflation and push down expensively valued tech stocks. For the ordinary wallet, it may eventually show up through fuel, transport and the prices of goods, even though the news itself starts far from the household budget.
