Hong Kong police have launched an artificial intelligence-powered national security education platform for young students.

The NSmarties AI Interactive Platform was officially launched on October 6, 2026, at a ceremony at Precious Blood Primary School in Ap Lei Chau, with about 700 pupils attending, police said in a Facebook post :

The platform, jointly developed by police and Hong Kong Qianfan Technology, lets students generate personalized stories centered on China’s “holistic approach to national security” — a framework pushed by Chinese President Xi Jinping that covers areas from politics and the economy to ecology and technology.

It also features the voices of Chow and security chief Chris Tang, who explain the city’s national security laws and security information.

The ceremony marked a “milestone” in bringing national security education into the city’s schools through innovative technologies, police said.

The NSmarties platform was first unveiled on National Security Education Day in April, but it was not fully launched to the public at that time.

Hong Kong has strengthened national security education alongside a crackdown since Beijing imposed a national security law in 2020 following the 2019 protests and unrest.

Beijing inserted national security legislation directly into Hong Kong’s mini-constitution in June 2020 following a year of pro-democracy protests and unrest. It criminalized subversion, secession, collusion with foreign forces and terrorist acts — broadly defined to include disruption to transport and other infrastructure. The move gave police sweeping new powers and led to hundreds of arrests amid new legal precedents , while dozens of civil society groups disappeared . The authorities say it restored stability and peace to the city, rejecting criticism from trade partners , the UN and NGOs .
