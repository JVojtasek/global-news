## BRIEFLY

**What happened.** The Bank of England kept Bank Rate at 3.75% but replaced annual decisions on quantitative tightening with a plan intended to finish the monetary-policy gilt unwind by September 2034.

**What it means.** Of the £488 billion portfolio, £120 billion of long-dated gilts will be retained to back banknotes. The remaining £368 billion will fall through £222 billion of maturities and £146 billion of sales. The Bank plans £20 billion of active sales a year, for an average total reduction of £46 billion including maturities.

**Risks and impact.** The Bank has paused its own auctions while it considers whether future sales should pass through the government’s Debt Management Office. That may change market pressure and execution risk, but not the direction of policy.

**What to watch.** By April 2027, the Bank says it will announce how the sales will operate.

## FACTS

The Monetary Policy Committee voted unanimously for the new multi-year QT plan. It separately voted 6–3 to hold Bank Rate at 3.75%; three members preferred 4%. Those are distinct decisions. The first concerns the central bank’s stock of government bonds. The second sets the short-term policy rate.

The Bank’s gilt holdings peaked at £895 billion in February 2022 and stood at £488 billion on September 16. It will designate £120 billion of the longest-dated gilts as banknote backing. That leaves £368 billion held for monetary-policy purposes.

The Bank expects £222 billion of that remainder to mature by 2034. It plans to sell the other £146 billion at £20 billion a year. Combined, maturities and sales are expected to reduce the monetary-policy stock by an average £46 billion a year.

Bank-run auctions are paused while officials consider sales to the government, potentially executed through the Debt Management Office. The final arrangement has not been decided. Operational details are promised by April 2027.

## EVIDENCE

The Bank’s minutes provide the portfolio arithmetic, the vote and the deadline. They also explain the policy logic: Bank Rate remains the active tool for changing monetary conditions, while bond sales should be gradual, predictable and conducted without disrupting markets.

The Bank estimates QT has added roughly 20–30 basis points to long-term gilt term premia. That is modest beside the roughly 200-basis-point rise it attributes mainly to global uncertainty, heavier government issuance and structural changes in demand. Reuters cited analysts with higher estimates for 30-year yields.

HM Treasury’s publication confirms that the Chancellor and Governor agreed both the future implementation work and the new banknote-backing arrangement. Reuters reported a strong rally in long-dated gilts after the announcement. The market reaction is evidence that execution matters, not proof that the policy stance suddenly became easy.

The uncertainty is operational. Officials are still deciding whether the government should buy bonds from the Asset Purchase Facility and retire or refinance them through the DMO. The destination—zero monetary-policy gilt holdings by 2034—was decided. The route was not.

### Where they agree

This is a redesign of balance-sheet plumbing with real market effects. It is neither a rate cut nor an abandonment of QT. The unresolved issue is how the Bank, Treasury and DMO preserve a visible line between monetary-policy decisions and debt-management execution.

## CONTEXT

Quantitative easing created reserves and used them to buy gilts. QT reverses the portfolio side through maturities and sales. It does not mechanically reverse every effect of QE or move in lockstep with Bank Rate.

Britain’s case is unusually sensitive because its government debt has a longer average maturity than that of many peers. Active sales therefore placed more long-dated bonds into a market already absorbing heavy public issuance. The Bank says markets remained orderly, but it also recognised that some holdings would become less aligned with demand as the unwind continued.

The £120 billion banknote portfolio can sound like QE by another name. Institutionally, it is different. Physical currency is a Bank of England liability and needs assets behind it. The retained gilts are being assigned to that enduring function, while the MPC’s temporary monetary-policy portfolio is scheduled to reach zero.

That accounting distinction will still need public scrutiny. The same bonds remain on the consolidated public balance sheet, and the Treasury indemnifies the Asset Purchase Facility. Changing timing, ownership and sales channels can shift cash flows and market supply even when it does not erase the underlying public-sector exposure.

## DEEPER

Central-bank balance sheets cannot be read through one number. A £488 billion portfolio contains assets serving different purposes, maturing on different dates and reaching markets through different institutions. Relabelling £120 billion matters because its purpose changes; pausing auctions matters because the unwind may resume through another channel.

For households and businesses, the immediate message is narrower than the bond-market move. Bank Rate stayed at 3.75%, and the Bank warned that inflation risks remain tilted upward. Mortgage pricing and corporate borrowing respond to several forces—expected policy rates, gilt yields, funding costs and risk premia. A rally in long bonds can ease one pressure without guaranteeing cheaper borrowing across the economy.

For the public finances, execution matters because the government is simultaneously issuing debt and covering APF cash losses. Coordinating sales may reduce the chance that two public institutions place similar bonds into the market at awkward moments. Coordination becomes problematic only if it lets fiscal convenience override the MPC’s monetary decision.

The cleanest reading is therefore procedural: the Bank has made the destination more predictable and the route less settled. Markets know the intended annual pace and end date. They do not yet know exactly who will conduct sales after April, how the DMO would incorporate them, or how the arrangement will be reported.

## IMPACT

The practical impact is greatest in the gilt market, where reduced long-dated supply pressure may affect pension funds, insurers and government financing. For everyone else, the useful guardrail is conceptual: do not translate “auction pause” into “interest-rate relief.”

The next decision point is April 2027. The durable evidence will be the operating framework, the DMO remit, published transaction data and whether the £20 billion annual sales commitment survives contact with volatile markets.

## READER OUTCOME

**What changed** — The Bank set a 2034 endpoint, separated £120 billion for banknote backing and paused its own auctions while a government-sale route is assessed.

**Why it matters** — The redesign can alter gilt supply and borrowing conditions without changing Bank Rate or cancelling QT.

**What to watch, not what to do** — Watch the April 2027 operating plan, the DMO’s role and whether actual sales match the promised path.

**What would change our minds** — A retreat from the £20 billion sales pace for fiscal convenience would weaken the claim that only implementation changed; transparent execution under the MPC’s fixed target would strengthen it.
