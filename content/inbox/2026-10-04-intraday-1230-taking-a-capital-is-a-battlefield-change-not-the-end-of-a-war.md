## BRIEFLY

**What happened.** Ethiopian federal forces and allied militias took control of Mekelle, the capital of Tigray, according to Reuters and AFP reporting from sources in the city. The TPLF said it had withdrawn and would temporarily operate the regional government from elsewhere.

**What it means.** This is a major territorial and political reversal after Tigrayan forces opened an offensive on September 23. It establishes who holds the regional capital now. It does not establish that the wider war is over.

**Risks and impact.** Public offices and police stations were reported unguarded during the transition. Communications remain severely disrupted, and the conflict is entangled with a wider diplomatic rupture involving Ethiopia and Eritrea.

**What can be done.** The useful distinction is between taking a city and consolidating control: administration, public order, road access and repeated military behaviour will show whether the change lasts.

**What to watch.** Watch for verified control beyond central Mekelle, protection of civilians and public property, restored communications, the TPLF's next operations and any return to direct talks.

## FACTS

Reuters reported on October 4 that federal troops and allied militias controlled Mekelle. Its account relied on a source close to the pro-government Tigray Peace Forces, a humanitarian source and a resident. The TPLF supplied the most important confirmation from the losing side: in a public statement, it said it had retreated and that Tigray's regional government would temporarily operate from another location.

AFP separately reported that federal forces entered the city on Saturday. A journalist said TPLF fighters and leaders were leaving, while a humanitarian source cited staff observations of federal and allied forces in several locations. Neither Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's office nor the Ethiopian military had publicly answered the outlets' requests for comment.

The capture followed an earlier, narrower change. AP confirmed federal control of Mekelle's airport but said on Saturday that citywide control was still unclear. That sequence matters: airport control, forces entering the centre and an opponent's acknowledged withdrawal are separate pieces of evidence that accumulated over time.

## EVIDENCE

The strongest verified conclusion is that the balance of control in Mekelle has changed. Reuters has confirmation from three kinds of sources, AFP has reporting from a journalist and humanitarian channel, and the TPLF acknowledges withdrawal. Together they support more than a claim by one belligerent.

The evidence is weaker on the condition of the city and the reach of federal control. Severe phone and internet disruption limits independent observation. AFP reported empty government offices and police stations, while residents feared looting and lawlessness. Those reports identify a risk during transition, not proof that widespread looting has occurred.

Nor do the available sources establish the destruction or surrender of the TPLF's forces. Its leader had earlier suggested a return to mobile attacks. That is a statement of intent, not proof of future capability. The location of the regional administration, the strength of forces outside Mekelle and the status of roads north and west of the city remain uncertain.

## CONTEXT

The capture reverses the TPLF's September offensive, when Tigrayan forces seized Mekelle, Axum and Shire airports and fighting spread into neighbouring areas. On September 23, the African Union said those airport seizures breached commitments under the Pretoria peace agreement and called for an immediate halt, direct talks and renewed mediation.

The 2022 Pretoria agreement ended the previous civil war's large-scale fighting but left major disputes over security, territory and political authority unresolved. The current fighting has exposed how incomplete that settlement remained.

History also cautions against reading city control as a final verdict. Federal troops captured Mekelle in 2020. The TPLF then fought an insurgent campaign and retook the city in 2021. The comparison is not a prediction that the same sequence will repeat. It identifies a mechanism: an armed movement can lose an urban centre and retain forces elsewhere.

The surroundings are now more dangerous. Reuters reports that Ethiopia accuses Eritrea of supporting the rebel alliance, which Eritrea denies, while Sudan and Egypt also deny Ethiopian allegations of involvement. Ethiopia and Eritrea have severed diplomatic ties. The United States has urged restraint and dialogue, warning that cross-border tension could affect the wider region.

## DEEPER

A capital performs several jobs at once. It is a seat of government, a communications hub, a transport node and a symbol. Capturing it changes each of those systems, but at different speeds.

The flag can change before payrolls, policing and public services do. Soldiers can occupy a centre before they secure roads. A regional government can leave its offices yet continue issuing orders from elsewhere. That is why “who holds the capital?” and “who governs the region?” are related questions, not identical ones.

The next phase will be legible through institutions rather than declarations. If federal authorities establish public order, protect civilians, reopen access and sustain control beyond the city, the capture will look like consolidation. If the TPLF preserves command, contests routes and mounts repeated operations, it will look more like a change in the war's form.

## IMPACT

For people in Mekelle, the immediate issue is the gap between one armed authority leaving and another establishing accountable control. Disrupted communications make both risk assessment and family contact harder. The sources do not support general evacuation, travel or stockpiling advice; conditions and reliable local guidance may vary sharply.

For outside readers, the practical test is layered: city centre, institutions, roads, services and opponent capacity. Treat each as a separate claim and update only when evidence reaches it.

## READER OUTCOME

**What changed** — Federal forces and allies took Mekelle, and the TPLF acknowledged withdrawing the regional administration.

**Why it matters** — The federal side now holds Tigray's political centre, reversing the rebels' recent offensive and weakening the TPLF-led alliance.

**What remains uncertain** — Control outside the city, the condition of civilians and services, the TPLF's remaining force and the extent of foreign involvement.

**What to watch, not what to do** — Watch independent reporting on public order, road control, communications, aid access, new TPLF operations and AU-backed talks.

**What would change our minds** — Sustained administration and uncontested transport links would support a consolidation reading. Repeated coordinated attacks or a renewed withdrawal from Mekelle would support a temporary-control reading.
