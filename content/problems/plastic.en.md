machine:
  optimise: "The single number is the recycling rate of plastic packaging waste at the calculation point: tonnes entering a recycling machine, divided by tonnes of plastic packaging placed on the market. Drive it up, and nothing else counts."
  moves:
    - "Do bottles first and film last. A PET bottle is one polymer, clean, uniform and worth money; a multilayer sachet is four polymers, dirty and worth nothing. Norway's deposit system returns 92.3% of drinks containers; nothing returns film at that rate. A calculator buying tonnes into the numerator buys the cheap tonnes, whatever is actually in the river."
    - "Feed the machine before shrinking the market. One extra tonne into a recycling plant raises the EU rate by 1 part in 15.8 million. One tonne of packaging never sold raises it by 0.421 of that, because the tonne leaves the bottom of the fraction and the top stays where it was. At a 42.1% rate, recycling a tonne is worth 2.4 tonnes not sold."
    - "Count the export. Waste sent abroad enters the numerator at the point it enters a recycling operation there, on documentary evidence of broadly equivalent conditions. Around 1.3 million tonnes of EU plastic waste went outside the EU in 2023. A document is cheaper than a sorting line."
    - "Ignore everything that is not packaging. The denominator is packaging placed on the market, so tyres, fishing gear, paint, synthetic clothing and agricultural film are outside the fraction entirely. In the EU, tyre abrasion releases rose 12.3% between 2016 and 2022. The rate does not move."
  arithmetic: "EU 2023: 15.8 million tonnes of plastic packaging waste, 42.1% of it recycled — 6.65 million tonnes. The 2030 target is 55%. Two arithmetic routes there. Route A: recycle 8.69 million tonnes of the same 15.8, which is 2.04 million tonnes more into machines. Route B: keep recycling 6.65 million tonnes and shrink the market to 12.09 million tonnes, which is 3.71 million tonnes of packaging never sold. Both land on 55%. The calculator picks whichever tonne is cheaper and has no opinion about which world it produces."

    text: "The bill for Europe's recycling rate has been paid in Adana and Istanbul. Human Rights Watch interviewed 64 people around Turkish plastic plants in 2022 — workers, residents and five children then working there — and recorded respiratory illness, headaches and skin complaints, with licensing and inspection rules unenforced. Türkiye took nearly half the EU's plastic waste exports in 2020 and 2021. None of that appears in the fraction; it is a tonne that entered a recycling operation."
  - point: "What the number flattens"
    text: "The rate is measured by weight, so a tonne of two-litre bottles and a tonne of two-gram sachets score identically — even though the second is half a million objects and the first is five hundred. Weight also hides what the recyclate becomes. A bottle turned back into a bottle can go round again; a bottle turned into a park bench is counted once and never returns. The calculation point records the entry, not the destination."
  - point: "What a spreadsheet cannot see"
    text: "The people who do most of the world's actual recycling are not in the dataset. Waste pickers in Lagos, Delhi and São Paulo recover material by hand, without contracts, safety equipment or a line in any national statistic, and a formal collection scheme can put them out of work while raising the reported rate. Nor does the rate see scale: UNEP counted 2.1 billion tonnes of municipal solid waste in 2023 and projects 3.8 billion by 2050. A percentage of a doubling is not the same problem twice."

A recycling rate can mean four different things, and the four are far apart. It can mean the plastic you put in the bin. It can mean the plastic a sorting plant pulls out of that bin. It can mean the plastic that gets on a ship. Or it can mean the plastic that goes into a machine and comes out as material. The OECD measured all four for the world in 2019: 15% of plastic waste was collected for recycling, about 40% of that was thrown away again as residue, and 9% was recycled. Half went to landfill, 19% was burned, and 22% was dumped, burned in the open or leaked.

This page uses the fourth definition, because it is the only one that is audited across countries. Since reference year 2020 the EU measures packaging recycling at the calculation point — the weight entering the recycling operation itself, after sorting losses come out. Reported rates dropped when the rule changed. Nothing changed in the bins. The EU average for plastic packaging was 42.1% in 2023, from 15.8 million tonnes generated, and it runs from 59.5% in Belgium to 23.0% in Hungary.

One honest hole remains, and it is large. Waste exported outside the EU counts as recycled if there is sound evidence it was handled under conditions broadly equivalent to Europe's. Around 1.3 million tonnes of EU plastic waste left in 2023, and the evidence is documentary. The global treaty that might have set common rules is still not written: talks collapsed in Busan in December 2024 and again in Geneva in August 2025, and the session on 7 February 2026 elected a new chair and negotiated nothing.
