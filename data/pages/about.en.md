# About The Deeper Story

Most news tells you what happened. Very little of it tells you what the thing
underneath is — the question a story opens up, the one you are still turning
over three hours later while doing something else entirely.

That gap is the whole reason this exists.

## How every article is built

Each piece moves through the same layers, in the same order:

**What happened** — the facts, as a wire service would report them. No framing,
no opinion. If something is unconfirmed, we say so in the sentence itself, not
in a footnote at the bottom.

**The background** — the history, the numbers, the context most coverage leaves
out. Including what is disputed and what nobody knows yet.

**Who it touches** — what this means for actual people rather than categories.

**The deeper story** — the part you came for. Not what to think about the news,
but which human question sits underneath it: worth, fear, security, justice,
power, loss, hope. We bring history, philosophy, psychology and the older wisdom
traditions to bear on that question, and we leave the conclusion to you.

**Something to sit with** — two or three questions. Nothing more.

## What we will not do

We will not tell you what to think. If a story has a point, you will find it
yourself, and you will trust it more for having found it.

We will not bury a bad story because we prefer good news. We report it, and then
we ask who is doing something about it.

We will not rewrite another outlet's article. We take facts, write our own words,
and link to the original every single time.

We will not use anyone's suffering as material.

## Where we are coming from

You are entitled to know who is behind what you read, so: the people who make
this work within the Christian tradition, and a portion of our longer pieces
draw on it openly — the same way another publication might draw on Stoicism,
Buddhism or secular humanism. It is one of the wisdom traditions we read, and
in those articles it is labelled as what it is.

What we will never do is smuggle it in. You will not find an argument that turns
into an appeal, or a news story that becomes a sermon in its final paragraph.
If we ever do that, we have failed at the one thing we are trying to be good at,
and you should stop reading us.

## When we get it wrong

We will. When we do, we correct it on the page itself, dated, and we leave the
correction visible. A quiet edit is a lie with better manners.

## How this is made

Parts of this newsroom are automated. Sources are gathered and cross-checked by
software, drafts are produced with the help of AI, and everything is measured
against a fixed set of editorial rules before publication. Sensitive categories —
deaths, accusations, elections — never publish without a human first. We think
you should know this, because you would want to know it about anyone else.
