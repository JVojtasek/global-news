## Slot 1 — Good News

- **Edition slot:** 1
- **Status:** draft
- **Role:** flagship
- **Type:** daily
- **Target length:** 1,400–2,000 words
- **Working title:** *Ten Human Cases, Hundreds of Animal Infections: Why the Last Guinea Worm Is the Hardest*
- **Reader question:** If Guinea worm disease is down to only a handful of human cases, why has eradication not already been declared?
- **Precise original angle:** Tell a hopeful public-health story without turning “almost gone” into “mission accomplished.” Use the record-low 2025 human count as the opening, then explain why animal reservoirs, long incubation, remote surveillance and the need to certify absence make the final kilometre harder than the first thousand. The success is not a pharmaceutical miracle: it is a decades-long system of safe water, filtering, case containment, incentives, surveillance and community trust.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** Eradication campaigns are often covered only when a milestone is announced. This piece gives readers a durable framework for understanding why the final cases of any infectious disease are disproportionately difficult and why patient, locally maintained systems matter.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** People acquire infection by drinking water containing infected copepods; after roughly 10–14 months an adult female worm emerges through the skin, and contact with water releases larvae that continue the cycle. Explain how filters, safe water, preventing infected people and animals from entering water sources, larvicide where appropriate, rewards and active surveillance interrupt different links.
- **Key uncertainties:** The 2025 count is provisional surveillance data; animal infections, especially in dogs and wildlife, complicate a human-only narrative; conflict and access gaps can hide cases; eradication certification requires sustained evidence of absence; no one should infer that low global numbers mean zero local risk.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Give readers a “last-mile test” for any eradication claim: What is counted, are animals involved, how long is the silent period, how complete is surveillance, and who verifies zero? Do not turn the article into generic charity promotion.
- **Recent-article check:** Accepted. No article in the 14-day inventory examined Guinea worm disease, disease eradication certification or animal reservoirs in an almost-eliminated human infection.

## Slot 2 — Travel

- **Edition slot:** 2
- **Status:** draft
- **Role:** evidence
- **Type:** analysis
- **Target length:** 1,000–1,500 words
- **Working title:** *Will Incognito Mode Really Find a Cheaper Flight?*
- **Reader question:** Do airlines raise a fare because they recognise you or see repeated searches, and can clearing cookies or using a private window produce a cheaper ticket?
- **Precise original angle:** Separate three mechanisms that travel advice routinely mixes together: ordinary fare-bucket inventory changes, stale or cached displays across sellers, and genuine personalised or surveillance pricing. Use the 2026 JetBlue controversy as a current test case, but do not treat an allegation as proof. The useful conclusion is not “airlines never personalise” or “cookies always cost you”; it is a repeatable comparison protocol that can reveal what actually changed.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** Airfare myths recur every holiday season. The distinction between inventory pricing, display differences and personalisation will remain useful even when airlines, sellers and algorithms change.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** Airlines sell seats through discrete fare classes with limited inventory; when a lower bucket closes, the displayed price can jump. Distribution systems and caches can update at different moments. Cookies and logged-in profiles can identify sessions, but identification alone does not prove that a higher fare was calculated for that individual.
- **Key uncertainties:** Airline revenue-management systems are proprietary; public experiments rarely control route, inventory, taxes, currency and timing simultaneously; the JetBlue questions were not a final regulatory finding; broader surveillance-pricing evidence does not establish the practice for a particular airline; loyalty or targeted offers may sometimes lower rather than raise a displayed price.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Provide a six-minute fare check: compare the same itinerary, passenger count, fare conditions, currency and total price at nearly the same time; use one logged-out comparison; save screenshots; judge the final checkout total, not the first headline fare. Treat incognito as a privacy/session tool, not a guaranteed discount.

## Slot 3 — Mysteries

- **Edition slot:** 3
- **Status:** draft
- **Role:** practical
- **Type:** analysis
- **Target length:** 900–1,400 words
- **Working title:** *The Family Story Test: How to Investigate a Legend Without Losing It*
- **Reader question:** How can you check a dramatic family story without insulting the person who told it—or mistaking a cherished memory for a verified fact?
- **Precise original angle:** Treat a family legend as both testimony and a research lead. The writer should preserve the narrator’s exact version first, then break it into testable claims and triangulate records created for different purposes. A contradiction need not “destroy” the story: it may reveal how memory, identity, silence and later retelling reshaped it.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** Families will keep inheriting stories about migration, war, hidden parentage, occupations and lost property. The method works across countries and record systems and creates a more honest archive for the next generation.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** Autobiographical memory is reconstructive and socially shaped; open questions can elicit richer accounts without planting details. Archival records are also imperfect because they were created by institutions for administrative purposes. Independent convergence across oral testimony, dates, places, certificates, censuses, military files, newspapers, letters and photographs raises confidence; a single match does not.
- **Key uncertainties:** Names and dates change across languages and clerks; archives have gaps and access restrictions; DNA can reveal unexpected relationships and privacy risks; absence from a database is not proof of absence; a psychologically meaningful family story can remain meaningful even when literal details are uncertain.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Build a one-page “claim ledger”: exact wording, teller and date, individual claims, supporting evidence, conflicting evidence, open questions and confidence level. Include consent and privacy checks before recording, sharing or using DNA services.
- **Recent-article check:** Accepted. The inventory contains historical mysteries and decipherments, but no practical guide to testing oral family history through archival triangulation.

- **Target length:** 1,000–1,600 words
- **Working title:** *How Orange Juice Became Breakfast—and Started Losing the Table*
- **Reader question:** Why did orange juice become a near-default breakfast drink in the United States, and why is that habit now weakening?
- **Precise original angle:** Reconstruct a manufactured tradition rather than a timeless one. Follow the chain from vitamin promotion and surplus fruit through wartime research, low-temperature concentration, cold chains, branded convenience and federal identity standards—then place it beside the modern decline in consumption and citrus-disease pressure. The glass on the breakfast table is a story of technology, logistics and marketing as much as taste.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** It gives readers a durable way to ask how an everyday food habit was created, standardised and normalised, instead of assuming popular routines are natural or permanent.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** Concentration removes water to reduce storage and transport volume; flavour can be protected or restored through lower-temperature processing and the addition of fresh juice components before freezing. A cold chain turns seasonal, regional fruit into a predictable national product. Standards of identity help define what may be sold under familiar names.
- **Key uncertainties:** Popular retellings compress multiple inventors and dates; wartime demand and postwar consumer adoption were related but not identical; “not from concentrate” is not synonymous with unprocessed; recent sales decline reflects several interacting factors, including prices, breakfast habits, sugar concerns and citrus greening.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Give readers a label decoder for concentrate, not-from-concentrate, juice drink and serving size, while avoiding moral judgments. Add a “manufactured habit” test they can apply to another staple: preservation technology, distribution, standards, advertising and social routine.
- **Memory timeline the writer must use:** Prewar concentrate quality problems; wartime research and demand; the 1940s low-temperature concentration breakthrough and patent; postwar branded expansion; federal product standards; recent production and consumption decline. Verify every date and distinguish invention, patent, commercial launch and mass adoption.

- **Working title:** *What Your VO₂ Max Number Really Means*
- **Reader question:** Is the VO₂ max shown by a watch a medical verdict, a fitness score, or a useful estimate that needs context?
- **Precise original angle:** Rescue a valuable measure from leaderboard culture. Explain what laboratory VO₂ measurement represents, why cardiorespiratory fitness is associated with long-term health, and why a wearable’s proprietary estimate is not interchangeable with direct gas analysis. Emphasise trends under similar conditions over a single flattering or alarming number.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** Watches will keep displaying cardio-fitness estimates, software will change, and readers will need a stable framework for interpreting the number without self-diagnosis.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** Oxygen consumption reflects the linked work of lungs, blood, heart, circulation and exercising muscle. Direct testing measures respiratory gases during graded exercise; consumer devices infer fitness from variables such as heart rate, speed, motion and user characteristics. Explain absolute versus body-mass-relative values and why weather, terrain, medication, sensor fit and algorithm assumptions can move an estimate.
- **Key uncertainties:** Large observational cohorts show association, not proof that raising the displayed number alone causes a particular outcome; laboratory protocols and reference norms differ; wearable validation samples are often small and selective; device errors can vary across fitness levels; proprietary algorithms and firmware change.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Give a safe interpretation ladder: check units and device conditions, compare like with like, use a rolling trend, pair it with symptoms and ordinary performance, and seek qualified assessment before using it for clinical decisions. Do not prescribe maximal testing to unassessed readers.

- **Working title:** *The Engineer Who Made One Click Do Three Jobs*
- **Reader question:** How did Nils Bohlin’s three-point seat belt turn a familiar restraint into a device ordinary people would actually use?
- **Precise original angle:** Make this a human design story rather than a saintly-inventor myth. Start with the 1959 patent and ask why geometry, one-handed use, comfort, vehicle anchoring, public acceptance and regulation mattered together. Bohlin’s contribution becomes more impressive when placed inside the larger safety system rather than credited with every life saved by seat belts.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** It is a timeless lesson in safety engineering: the best protection must fit the body, the machine and real human behaviour, and adoption matters as much as invention.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** A lap portion loads the pelvis while a diagonal portion helps restrain the torso; three anchor points spread forces and reduce forward movement while a single buckle supports everyday use. Explain why correct fit, pretensioners, load limiters, airbags, vehicle structure and rear-seat use still matter.
- **Key uncertainties:** Historical accounts simplify competing belt designs and adoption timelines; a patent proves the design and assignee but not every later claim about licensing policy; saved-life estimates depend on use rates and modelling; three-point belts reduce risk but do not eliminate injury.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Give readers a 30-second fit check—lap belt low across hips, shoulder belt across chest, no routing behind the back or under the arm, and proper child restraint for size and age—using official safety guidance rather than improvisation.

- **Working title:** *03:14:08: The Software Deadline Already Hiding in Old Machines*
- **Reader question:** What is the Year 2038 problem, which systems may still be exposed, and how can an organisation investigate without pretending that every modern device is a ticking bomb?
- **Precise original angle:** Replace countdown panic with an asset-and-interface audit. Many mainstream 64-bit platforms already have mitigations, but time values also live in old binaries, embedded firmware, database columns, file formats, authentication systems and cross-system APIs. The durable story is how a seemingly simple type change becomes a coordination problem across long-lived equipment.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** The deadline will keep approaching while embedded and industrial systems remain in service for years. Readers need a measured explanation and a repeatable audit method, not a one-day scare story.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** A signed 32-bit Unix time value counts seconds from 1970 and reaches its maximum at 2,147,483,647; the next second cannot be represented in the same signed type. Moving to wider time values solves the numeric range only when application binary interfaces, stored data, protocols and dependent components agree.
- **Key uncertainties:** Exposure cannot be inferred from a marketing label or processor width; some 32-bit systems have 64-bit time interfaces and some 64-bit applications still store narrow timestamps; vendor support and testability vary; failure modes range from rejected dates to incorrect sorting or authentication, not necessarily a total shutdown.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Provide a non-invasive audit: inventory long-lived systems and vendors, identify timestamps crossing 2038, check database and protocol fields, demand documented support, test future dates in a safe replica, verify rollback and backups, and record dependencies. Do not provide exploit instructions or advise changing production clocks.
