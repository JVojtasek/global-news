## Slot 1 — flagship · goodnews · daily · 1,400–2,000 words

**Status:** ASSIGNED AND DRAFTED

**Reader question:** How can a reef be recovering and still be at serious risk?

**Original angle:** Treat hope as a measured recovery process, not a declaration that the Great Barrier Reef is saved. Explain what coral cover measures, why fast-growing coral creates visible gains and volatility, and which local interventions can genuinely buy recovery time.

**Six-month value:** The four-question method for reading ecosystem recovery claims works with the next reef survey and with other restoration stories.

**Mechanism:** Bleaching, survival, recruitment, growth, repeated heat, crown-of-thorns predation, water quality and targeted stewardship.

**Key uncertainties:** Regional averages hide local and species changes; cover is not full ecosystem function; local management cannot substitute for climate mitigation.

**Opened sources:** [Reef Authority health updates](https://www.gbrmpa.gov.au/learn/reef-health/reef-health-updates), [current independent reporting](https://www.theaustralian.com.au/science/great-resilient-reef-reports-of-wonders-death-have-been-wildly-exaggerated/news-story/b028c9622ff0f89aca2cc98d1e6e5e56), [AIMS annual summary](https://www.aims.gov.au/monitoring-great-barrier-reef/gbr-condition-summary-2024-25), [Tourism Reef Protection Initiative](https://www.gbrmpa.gov.au/our-work/programs-and-projects/tourism-reef-protection-initiative), [Australian resilience programme](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/about/news/building-reef-resilience-through-targeted-action), [UNESCO site record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/154).

## Slot 2 — evidence · science · analysis · 1,000–1,500 words

**Status:** ASSIGNED AND DRAFTED

**Reader question:** Do blue-light glasses help eye strain, sleep or retinal health?

**Original angle:** Unbundle three claims that are marketed as one problem, then match each to its mechanism and evidence.

**Six-month value:** The reader learns a reusable test for products that borrow credibility from a real mechanism without demonstrating a useful outcome.

**Mechanism:** Blinking and near focus for eye strain; total evening light and circadian timing for sleep; dose and long-term outcomes for retinal claims.

**Key uncertainties:** Small and heterogeneous sleep studies; differing lens strength; no long-term macular outcomes in the broad review.

**Opened sources:** [Cochrane evidence review](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD013244_blue-light-filtering-spectacle-lenses-visual-performance-macular-back-part-eye-protection-and), [Cochrane summary](https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/blue-light-filtering-spectacles-probably-make-no-difference-eye-strain-eye-health-or-sleep), [computer-task trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33707105/), [sleep meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41341515/).

## Slot 3 — practical · history · analysis · 900–1,400 words

**Status:** ASSIGNED AND DRAFTED

**Reader question:** What should a household do during the first four hours of a power outage?

**Original angle:** Use the cascading failures of the 2003 North American blackout to teach household dependency mapping, without disaster spectacle or shopping-list fear.

**Six-month value:** A calm first-four-hours protocol remains useful for storms, equipment failures and grid incidents.

**Mechanism:** Cascading dependencies, situational awareness, food temperature, communications, medical-device backup and carbon-monoxide exposure.

**Key uncertainties:** Outage length and local infrastructure vary; medical-device plans must be individual and professionally documented.

**Opened sources:** [FERC blackout archive](https://www.ferc.gov/industries-data/electric/overview/electric-reliability/power-blackout), [FERC reliability standards](https://www.ferc.gov/commissioner-kellys-statement-mandatory-reliability-standards-bulk-power-system), [CDC outage guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/response/what-to-do-protect-yourself-during-a-power-outage.html), [CDC power checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/prepare-your-health/take-action/power-sources.html), [CDC food safety](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/psa-toolkit/discarding-food-after-a-power-outage.html).

## Slot 4 — memory · business · analysis · 1,000–1,600 words

**Status:** ASSIGNED AND DRAFTED

**Reader question:** Does a four-day week still work after the novelty wears off?

**Original angle:** Separate reduced hours from four compressed long days and show that successful trials redesigned work before deleting calendar time.

**Six-month value:** The baseline, redesign, pilot and guardrail method can guide a real team decision long after the current workplace debate.

**Mechanism:** Work reorganisation, recovery time, sleep and fatigue, hidden overtime, service metrics and workload intensification.

**Key uncertainties:** Voluntary organisations, self-report, role differences, limited shared productivity measures and longer-term durability.

**Opened sources:** [Nature Human Behaviour study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02259-6), [Nature research report](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-02295-2), [Scottish public-sector pilot](https://www.gov.scot/policies/public-sector-pay/four-day-working-week/), [Iceland shorter-week report](https://en.alda.is/2021/07/04/going-public-icelands-journey-to-a-shorter-working-week/), [ABC independent reporting](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-07-22/four-day-work-week-health-burnout/105555392).

## Slot 5 — evergreen · AI · analysis · 1,000–1,500 words

**Status:** ASSIGNED AND DRAFTED

**Reader question:** Why does an AI assistant agree with a user's premise even when it is wrong?

**Original angle:** Explain sycophancy as a training and feedback problem, then give readers adversarial prompts that invite correction rather than reassurance.

**Six-month value:** The technique remains useful across model versions.

**Mechanism:** Preference optimisation, agreement cues, confirmation pressure and independent verification.

**Key uncertainties:** Behaviour varies by model, prompt and update; a confident disagreement is not proof of truth.

**Opened sources:** [Sharma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548), [Wei et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03958), [OpenAI incident report](https://openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/), [OpenAI follow-up](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/).

## Slot 6 — human · technology · analysis · 900–1,400 words

**Status:** ASSIGNED AND DRAFTED

**Reader question:** Who preserves a video game after its store, server or original machine disappears?

**Original angle:** Follow the code, hardware, documentation and human memory that must all survive for an interactive work to remain understandable.

**Six-month value:** The piece gives readers a durable framework for preservation, ownership and cultural access.

**Mechanism:** Platform dependency, rights exceptions, emulation, archival description and oral history.

**Key uncertainties:** Legal exceptions differ by jurisdiction and do not solve access to every work.

**Opened sources:** [Video Game History Foundation availability study](https://gamehistory.org/87percent/), [US copyright regulation](https://www.copyright.gov/title37/201/37cfr201-40.html), [The Strong museum collection](https://www.museumofplay.org/collections/icheg/), [VGHF library](https://gamehistory.org/vghf-library-launch/), [archive update](https://gamehistory.org/archive-portal-update-2026/).

## Slot 7 — reserve · science · feature · 1,000–1,600 words

**Status:** ASSIGNED AND DRAFTED — RESERVE

**Reader question:** Do trees communicate through shared fungal networks?

**Original angle:** Preserve the fascinating evidence for resource transfer and signalling while testing the stronger metaphor of a cooperative forest-wide social network.

**Six-month value:** It teaches how a valid experiment can acquire a more certain cultural story than its data justify.

**Mechanism:** Mycorrhizal connections, isotope tracing, transfer versus communication and citation amplification.

**Key uncertainties:** Network prevalence and ecological meaning in mature forests; experimental conditions; anthropomorphic language.

**Opened sources:** [Francis and Read](https://www.nature.com/articles/307053a0), [Simard et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/41557), [Song et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep08495), [Karst, Jones and Hoeksema](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-023-01986-1).
