## Slot 1 — The Heatwave Test Every Summer Trip Now Needs

- Slot: 1
- Section: travel
- Role: flagship
- Type: daily
- Status: draft
- Word range: 1,400–2,000
- Reader question: How should I change a city break or family holiday when the forecast shows dangerous heat, without either cancelling reflexively or treating the temperature as background scenery?
- Precise original angle: Build the story around a “heat itinerary audit” that treats the overnight minimum, humidity, shade, walking distance, accommodation cooling, medicine storage and flexible booking terms as one system. The useful comparison is not “hot destination versus cool destination” but “exposure that can be moved versus exposure that cannot.”
- Why it will remain useful in six months: Readers will face the same booking, packing and scheduling decisions every warm season. The framework also transfers across countries instead of expiring with one named heatwave.
- Mechanism the writer must explain: Human heat balance depends on metabolic heat, ambient temperature, radiation, air movement and evaporative cooling. Humidity reduces the usefulness of sweating; cities retain heat after sunset; acclimatisation is gradual; dehydration and some medicines can narrow the safety margin.
- Key uncertainties: Individual vulnerability varies with age, pregnancy, fitness, illness, medicines and acclimatisation. Weather forecasts change. Air-conditioning quality, urban shade and emergency capacity vary by building and destination. Avoid universal temperature cut-offs and medical diagnosis.
- Practical deliverable: A booking-stage checklist, a 24-hour itinerary rewrite and clear red flags for seeking local medical help.

## Slot 2 — Can Your Handwriting Reveal Your Personality?

- Slot: 2
- Section: mysteries
- Role: evidence
- Type: analysis
- Status: draft
- Word range: 1,000–1,500
- Reader question: Can a trained graphologist really infer personality from handwriting, and why do many descriptions still feel uncannily accurate?
- Precise original angle: Put two superficially similar practices side by side: forensic examination can compare questioned writing for authorship or alteration, while graphology claims to infer personality. Test the second claim without dismissing the reader’s experience of recognition. Explain how access to the text’s content, broad statements and selective memory can make a weak method feel personal.
- Why it will remain useful in six months: Handwriting personality tests repeatedly return in recruitment, social media and entertainment. The article teaches a reusable method for evaluating any personality-reading service.
- Mechanism the writer must explain: Predictive validity, blinded versus unblinded samples, content leakage, the Barnum effect and confirmation bias. Explain why “this description feels like me” is not the same as independent prediction.
- Key uncertainties: Handwriting can reflect motor constraints and temporary conditions, and forensic document examination answers a different question. Some older graphology studies are small or methodologically uneven. Do not claim handwriting contains no information whatsoever; keep the conclusion tied to personality inference.
- Practical deliverable: A five-question credibility test readers can apply before trusting a handwriting, face, voice or online personality reading.

## Slot 3 — The Olive Oil Shelf Test

- Slot: 3
- Section: food
- Role: practical
- Type: analysis
- Status: draft
- Word range: 900–1,400
- Reader question: Which bottle and storage choices actually help olive oil stay good, and which popular “freshness tests” tell me almost nothing?
- Precise original angle: Follow one bottle from shop shelf to the last tablespoon. Turn chemistry into four household decisions: container, bottle size, heat/light exposure and pace of use. Separate sensory decline from food-safety panic, and explain why a refrigerator “purity test” cannot authenticate an oil.
- Why it will remain useful in six months: The advice applies every time a reader buys or stores oil and supports durable search questions about dark bottles, expiry dates, refrigeration and rancidity.
- Mechanism the writer must explain: Oxygen, light and heat accelerate lipid oxidation; volatile aromas and phenolic compounds change during storage; headspace grows as a bottle empties; cultivar and starting composition alter stability.
- Key uncertainties: Harvest dates and best-before rules differ by market. A dark bottle cannot prove quality or authenticity. Laboratory findings under controlled storage do not yield one universal household shelf life. Sensory quality and legal grade are not interchangeable.
- Practical deliverable: A 60-second shop test and a home “use-it-before-it-fades” plan based on household consumption rather than a universal deadline.

## Slot 4 — How RICE Lost Its Monopoly on Sports-Injury Advice

- Slot: 4
- Section: sport
- Role: memory
- Type: analysis
- Status: draft
- Word range: 1,000–1,600
- Reader question: Why did “rest, ice, compression, elevation” become almost automatic advice, and what does today’s evidence actually support after a soft-tissue injury?
- Precise original angle: Tell the history of a memorable acronym becoming folk medicine, then follow the shift from RICE to PRICE, POLICE and PEACE & LOVE. The conclusion is not another slogan to obey: it is a decision map separating short-term pain relief, protection, early appropriate loading and injuries that require assessment.
- Why it will remain useful in six months: Sprains and strains are perennial. Readers need a stable way to understand changing guidance instead of chasing the latest acronym.
- Mechanism the writer must explain: Cooling can reduce pain and local blood flow, while prolonged rest can delay return of strength and function. Progressive loading supplies mechanical signals during repair, but timing and dose depend on injury severity and tissue.
- Key uncertainties: Evidence differs by injury, cooling protocol and outcome; much of it is not high quality. PEACE & LOVE began as an expert framework, not a universal treatment trial. A suspected fracture, major instability, deformity, neurovascular symptoms or inability to bear weight needs professional assessment.
- Practical deliverable: A “relief versus recovery” table plus questions to ask a clinician. Do not prescribe a personalised rehabilitation programme.

## Slot 5 — Why the Tyre-Pressure Light Comes On When the Weather Turns Cold

- Slot: 5
- Section: motoring
- Role: evergreen
- Type: analysis
- Status: draft
- Word range: 1,000–1,500
- Reader question: Does a cold-morning tyre-pressure warning mean a puncture, harmless weather physics or overdue maintenance—and which number should I trust?
- Precise original angle: Start with the common warning that disappears after driving. Explain why disappearance is not proof that no action is needed. Build a practical hierarchy: visible damage and rapid loss first, the vehicle placard’s cold pressure second, then a repeat measurement that distinguishes weather-related change from a leak.
- Why it will remain useful in six months: Seasonal temperature changes and tyre-pressure warnings recur every year. The article answers a durable ownership question for combustion, hybrid and electric cars alike.
- Mechanism the writer must explain: Gas pressure falls as temperature falls; driving warms the tyre and raises measured pressure; air also escapes gradually through permeation. TPMS is a warning system, not a substitute for measurement. The tyre sidewall maximum is not the normal vehicle setting.
- Key uncertainties: Recommended pressures vary by vehicle, load, tyre and market. Direct and indirect TPMS behave differently. A simple temperature rule is approximate, not a leak diagnosis.
- Practical deliverable: A five-minute cold-tyre check, including where to find the correct pressure and when persistent or unequal loss warrants inspection.

## Slot 6 — The Woman Beside the Code

- Slot: 6
- Section: tech
- Role: human
- Type: analysis
- Status: draft
- Word range: 900–1,400
- Reader question: What did Margaret Hamilton and her team actually change about software engineering, beyond the famous photograph and the simplified claim that one woman “saved Apollo”?
- Precise original angle: Use the towering printout photograph as the doorway, then widen the frame to the team, the flight computer, priority scheduling and the 1201/1202 alarms. Preserve Hamilton’s agency without turning collaborative engineering into a lone-genius myth.
- Why it will remain useful in six months: It is a durable human history of software reliability, useful whenever readers ask why systems should fail selectively rather than collapse completely.
- Mechanism the writer must explain: The Apollo guidance software prioritised essential work and could reject lower-priority tasks under overload. Reliable systems emerge from requirements, testing, error detection, operational procedures and teams—not merely from “bug-free code.”
- Key uncertainties: Popular accounts compress the contributions of many engineers and sometimes overstate the direct causal role of one design decision. Distinguish Hamilton’s own recollections, institutional history and later retellings. Avoid implying she personally wrote every line.
- Practical deliverable: Four design lessons for present-day product teams: prioritise, expose overload, rehearse failure and document human decisions.

## Slot 7 — The Sibling Meeting to Have Before a Parent Needs Care

- Slot: 7
- Section: relationships
- Role: reserve
- Type: feature
- Status: reserve
- Word range: 1,000–1,600
- Reader question: How can adult siblings divide future care for an ageing parent before distance, money and old family roles turn a crisis into a conflict?
- Precise original angle: Replace the vague ideal of an equal split with a negotiated “fairness map”: the parent’s wishes first, then time, proximity, money, skills, administration, emotional labour and respite. Centre one calm meeting held before an emergency, not a dramatic reconciliation after one.
- Why it will remain useful in six months: Ageing-parent care is a durable family transition. The meeting format can be revisited as needs change and can serve readers years after publication.
- Mechanism the writer must explain: Care work is often invisible and uneven. Geography, gender expectations, resources, expertise and earlier sibling roles shape who notices, coordinates and performs tasks. Shared information and explicit task ownership reduce ambiguity, but fairness does not always mean identical hours.
- Key uncertainties: Families differ in culture, finances, legal systems, disability and relationship safety. The parent must remain a participant wherever possible. Some families need a clinician, social worker, mediator or lawyer; an article cannot settle medical, financial or legal authority.
- Practical deliverable: A 45-minute meeting agenda, a task-and-backup table and a rule for revisiting the plan without keeping score.
