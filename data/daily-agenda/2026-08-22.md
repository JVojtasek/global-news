## Slot 1 — Food

- **Edition slot:** 1
- **Status:** draft
- **Role:** flagship
- **Type:** daily
- **Target length:** 1,400–2,000 words
- **Working title:** *The Salt Swap That Could Prevent Strokes — and the People Who Should Not Make It Alone*
- **Reader question:** Can replacing ordinary salt with a potassium-enriched lower-sodium substitute meaningfully reduce cardiovascular risk, and who needs medical advice first?
- **Precise original angle:** Treat “use less salt” as a formulation problem rather than a willpower lecture. Explain why partially replacing sodium chloride with potassium chloride may lower blood pressure and cardiovascular events, then put the strongest result beside its limits: the outcome evidence is dominated by one large rural Chinese trial, while kidney impairment and medicines that raise potassium can change the safety equation.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** Salt substitutes will remain on supermarket shelves, hypertension will remain common, and readers need a durable way to distinguish a promising population intervention from a universal individual recommendation.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** Sodium and potassium influence fluid balance, vascular tone and blood pressure. A substitute can reduce sodium while increasing potassium without requiring people to abandon the salty taste entirely. Explain why blood-pressure effects are more generalisable than claims about deaths or strokes, and why impaired potassium excretion can make the same product unsafe for some people.
- **Key uncertainties:** Cardiovascular-outcome evidence is geographically concentrated; discretionary salt use differs between countries; product formulations vary; trials often exclude or underrepresent people at greatest hyperkalaemia risk; “no excess serious hyperkalaemia detected” in one trial is not proof of universal safety.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Give readers a label-reading test for sodium and potassium, a clear “ask first” list covering kidney disease and potassium-affecting medicines, and alternatives for households that should not use potassium-enriched salt. Do not diagnose or recommend stopping medication.
- **Recent-article check:** Accepted. The nearest recent food article examined eating speed and texture; this assignment concerns electrolyte formulation, blood pressure, clinical outcomes and contraindications.

## Slot 2 — Motoring

- **Edition slot:** 2
- **Status:** draft
- **Role:** evidence
- **Type:** analysis
- **Target length:** 1,000–1,500 words
- **Working title:** *Are Electric Cars Really Cleaner? The Boundary Test Behind Every Confident Answer*
- **Reader question:** Once battery production and electricity generation are counted, is an electric car still better for the climate than a petrol or diesel car?
- **Precise original angle:** Do not answer with one global percentage. Teach readers to audit the boundary of any lifecycle claim: vehicle and battery manufacture, fuel or electricity production, use, maintenance, lifetime mileage, vehicle size and end of life. Use a worked comparison to show how the answer changes with the local grid and how quickly a production “carbon debt” can be repaid.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** Marketing claims and national grids will change, but the boundary test lets readers evaluate any future lifecycle comparison.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** Battery manufacture can give a battery-electric vehicle higher production emissions, while the absence of tailpipe combustion and more efficient electric propulsion usually reduce use-phase emissions. Lifetime results depend on the electricity mix, vehicle and battery size, distance driven and assumed service life.
- **Key uncertainties:** Lifecycle databases and recycling credits differ; marginal electricity can differ from annual grid averages; real-world consumption changes with temperature and driving; large vehicles can erase part of the advantage; European estimates cannot be pasted onto every country.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Provide a five-input checklist readers can use with an official calculator: country/grid, comparable vehicle size, battery size, annual kilometres and expected ownership/lifetime. Separate climate emissions from local air pollution and mineral impacts.
- **Recent-article check:** Accepted. A recent motoring piece tested fuel-economy displays and combustion-car real-world CO2; it did not compare full EV and combustion-vehicle lifecycles.

## Slot 3 — Soul

- **Edition slot:** 3
- **Status:** draft
- **Role:** practical
- **Type:** analysis
- **Target length:** 900–1,400 words
- **Working title:** *When Exhaustion Is Not the Whole Story: A Practical Guide to Moral Injury*
- **Reader question:** What if work feels unbearable not only because there is too much of it, but because it repeatedly asks you to violate, witness or be betrayed over deeply held values?
- **Precise original angle:** Distinguish ordinary guilt, burnout, moral distress, moral injury and PTSD without turning any of them into a self-diagnosis. Anchor the article in everyday high-responsibility settings—health care, emergency work, military service, social care and leadership—and show why rest alone may not repair a wound organised around meaning, responsibility or betrayal.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** People will continue to mislabel every form of occupational suffering as burnout. A careful vocabulary can improve reflection, conversations with employers and decisions about professional help.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** A perceived transgression, omission, witnessed act or institutional betrayal can collide with a person’s moral framework. Shame, guilt, anger, loss of trust and social withdrawal may then reinforce one another. Acknowledgment, context, accountability, social connection and values-consistent action may matter alongside workload reduction.
- **Key uncertainties:** Moral injury is not itself a formal diagnosis; definitions and measures vary; most evidence is associative and concentrated in military or health-care populations; treatment evidence remains limited; symptoms can overlap with depression, PTSD and burnout.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Offer a non-diagnostic reflection: What happened? Which value was crossed? What was under your control? What repair is possible? Who can acknowledge the event safely? Include clear guidance to seek qualified help when distress, impaired functioning or thoughts of self-harm persist.
- **Recent-article check:** Accepted. Yesterday’s soul article corrected the five-stage grief checklist; recent pieces on burnout focused on workload and boundaries, not value violation or institutional betrayal.

## Slot 4 — Good News

- **Edition slot:** 4
- **Status:** draft
- **Role:** memory
- **Type:** analysis
- **Target length:** 1,000–1,600 words
- **Working title:** *The Ozone Layer Is Healing: The Long Repair Job Behind a Rare Global Success*
- **Reader question:** How did the world move from discovering an expanding ozone threat to measurable recovery, and why is the job still unfinished?
- **Precise original angle:** Tell the story as a repair system, not a miracle: scientific detection, a treaty with scheduled controls, financial and technical support, repeated tightening, atmospheric monitoring and industry substitution. Contrast the near-global phase-out of controlled ozone-depleting substances with the slower physical recovery of the atmosphere.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** The Montreal Protocol is a durable case study in how environmental cooperation can work—and in why policy success and physical recovery run on different clocks.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** Chlorine- and bromine-containing compounds persist long enough to reach the stratosphere, where ultraviolet light releases reactive species that destroy ozone catalytically. Cutting emissions stops adding to the burden, but long atmospheric lifetimes mean recovery takes decades. Explain how the Kigali Amendment also addresses climate-warming substitutes.
- **Key uncertainties:** Recovery dates are projections, not appointments; Antarctic variability is large; lower-stratospheric trends remain less clear in some regions; illegal or unexpected emissions and proposed geoengineering could alter the path; one successful treaty does not transfer automatically to every global problem.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Give readers a five-step “what worked” framework—measurable harm, replaceable chemicals, binding schedules, assistance, monitoring—and identify where that framework does and does not apply elsewhere.
- **Memory timeline the writer must use:** 1987 Montreal Protocol adoption; 1989 entry into force; 2016 Kigali Amendment adoption; 2022 scientific assessment; 2024 ozone-hole monitoring. Verify each date from the sources and explain what changed versus what remained slow.
- **Recent-article check:** Accepted. Recent good-news pieces covered community borrowing, food rescue, salmon and reefs; none analysed ozone recovery or the Montreal Protocol.

## Slot 5 — Travel

- **Edition slot:** 5
- **Status:** draft
- **Role:** evergreen
- **Type:** analysis
- **Target length:** 1,000–1,500 words
- **Working title:** *Turbulence Without the Panic: What Is Moving, What Can Hurt You and What the Seat Belt Changes*
- **Reader question:** Is turbulence a threat to the aircraft, a threat to an unbelted passenger, or both?
- **Precise original angle:** Split a frightening experience into three different risks: aircraft control and structure, passenger movement inside the cabin, and uncertainty in forecasting. Start with the physics of changing airflow, then show why the most practical risk control is mundane—remaining belted whenever seated—without promising that forecasting or climate models can eliminate surprises.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** Turbulence anxiety and seat-belt decisions recur on every flight; the physics and injury mechanism are more durable than viral footage.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** Wind shear, convection, terrain and jet-stream dynamics can create rapid changes in vertical and horizontal airflow. The aircraft moves with the air mass, while an unrestrained body can continue on its previous path relative to the cabin. Clear-air turbulence may occur without visible clouds.
- **Key uncertainties:** Injury reporting is incomplete and definitions vary; rare extreme events dominate attention; climate studies model statistical changes in clear-air turbulence rather than predicting one flight; weather, route and aircraft operations affect exposure.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Explain a calm passenger protocol: keep the belt low and snug whenever seated, secure loose objects, follow crew instructions, and ask an airline or clinician about individual mobility or medical needs before travel. Do not imply that passengers should ignore evacuation commands.
- **Recent-article check:** Accepted. Recent travel coverage addressed border systems, stamps and ticket pricing; no recent article explained turbulence physics and passenger injury prevention.

## Slot 6 — Mysteries

- **Edition slot:** 6
- **Status:** draft
- **Role:** human
- **Type:** analysis
- **Target length:** 900–1,400 words
- **Working title:** *The Architect, the Index Cards and the Ancient Script: How Linear B Was Really Deciphered*
- **Reader question:** How did an architect help turn an unreadable Bronze Age script into Greek—and whose painstaking work made the breakthrough possible?
- **Precise original angle:** Use Michael Ventris’s visual and architectural habits as the human entry, then dismantle the lone-genius myth. Show how Alice Kober’s hand-sorted evidence established inflectional patterns, how Emmett Bennett’s sign work helped structure the data, and how Ventris used grids, place-name hypotheses and falsifiable predictions to reach a reading others could test.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** It is a timeless story about pattern recognition, collaboration, intellectual humility and how a genuine decipherment earns trust.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** Repeated sign groups and changing endings can reveal grammatical structure before sounds are known. A syllabic grid groups signs by shared consonant or vowel relationships. Tentative sound values and Cretan place names generated readings that could be checked against tablets not used to build the hypothesis.
- **Key uncertainties:** Historical credit has often been simplified; some accounts romanticise intuition; archive interpretation continues; Kober died before the decipherment and claims about what she “would have solved” are counterfactual; distinguish Linear B from still-undeciphered Linear A.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** End with a reader’s “real decipherment” test: does the proposal explain many signs consistently, predict unseen text, survive independent checking and avoid cherry-picked resemblance?
- **Recent-article check:** Accepted. Recent mysteries covered pareidolia, the Antikythera mechanism, Jericho and the Somerton Man; none covered Linear B, Ventris or Kober.

## Slot 7 — Sport

- **Edition slot:** 7
- **Status:** reserve
- **Role:** reserve
- **Type:** feature
- **Target length:** 1,000–1,600 words
- **Working title:** *The Birthday Bias Hidden in Youth Sport*
- **Reader question:** Can a child look more “talented” simply because the selection calendar made them almost a year older than a teammate?
- **Precise original angle:** Follow two fictional children born at opposite ends of the same eligibility year through one selection cycle. Explain how an arbitrary cutoff can be mistaken for talent, then examine evidence across sports and levels rather than presenting the effect as universal or destiny.
- **Why it will remain useful in six months:** Youth selection cycles repeat annually, and parents, coaches and clubs need a durable way to separate current maturity from long-term potential.
- **Mechanism the writer must explain:** Within one age band, several months can matter for size, strength, coordination and confidence. Early selection can bring more coaching, competition and expectation, creating cumulative advantage; deselection can reduce opportunity. The effect changes with age, sport, participation level and maturation and may weaken or reverse among survivors.
- **Key uncertainties:** Effect sizes are usually small at population level but can matter at selection margins; birth patterns and cutoff rules differ; biological maturity is not identical to relative age; some interventions merely move the bias; observational distributions do not prove what happened to one child.
- **Practical takeaway to earn:** Give parents and coaches four questions: What is the cutoff? How are birth quarters represented? Are players assessed more than once and in different contexts? Does the programme distinguish maturation from skill? Avoid labelling an individual child as disadvantaged from birth date alone.
- **Recent-article check:** Accepted. Recent sport articles covered cold-water recovery, daily steps, transfer fees and Premier League competition; none examined relative-age selection bias.
